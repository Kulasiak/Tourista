Pilgrimage to Lourdes: what to know before you go

How much walking there is, how the water gesture works, what to pack and how to organise wheelchairs.

Urania · 2026-06-02 · 5 min read

Lourdes is a different kind of trip: people go for a reason that is not tourism, and whoever organises it has to think about things nobody usually thinks about. Here is what we are asked most.

How much walking. Inside the Sanctuary the distances are short but constant: from the Grotto to the basilica of St Pius X is five minutes, and in one day you do that several times. Those who walk slowly must be able to go back to the hotel and rest without depending on a shuttle: that is why we choose hotels within three hundred metres of the Grotto.

Wheelchairs. The Sanctuary provides them free of charge, but you have to ask at the entrance and in busy periods they run out. If there are people in the group who need one, tell us when you book: our coach has a lift and we reserve the front seats.

The baths. Immersion has been suspended for some years and replaced by the water gesture: you go along in line and receive water on your hands and face. The queues move faster than they used to, but they are still long in the middle of the day: go early in the morning.

The torchlight procession. Every evening at nine, and it is the moment everyone remembers. You buy the candles there, they cost little. Bring a jacket: even in July, in the evening, the cool comes down from the Pyrenees.

In the suitcase. Comfortable shoes already worn in, a light raincoat (it rains often and suddenly), medicines in the hand luggage, and a bottle to fill at the fountains: the water is drinkable and free.

The pace. Our programme always leaves half a day free. That is not laziness: after two days of celebrations a group of elderly people needs to stop, and those who stop go home happy instead of exhausted.
